INTRODUCTION

REFLECTRE

REFLECTRE is an ongoing exploration of consciousness, perception, and the relationship between the inner and outer worlds.

Through charcoal, portraiture, symbolism, and atmospheric imagery, the series investigates memory, vulnerability, transformation, and spiritual experience.

Each work emerges from an attempt to observe the invisible structures that shape how we experience ourselves and the world around us.

Each work therefore invites the viewer to pause, observe, and recognise something of their own experience within the image.

02

REFLECTRE does not seek to define consciousness or offer a singular interpretation of existence. Instead, it creates a space for contemplation — a place where perception can become uncertain, where memory can overlap with imagination, and where the boundary between the internal and external begins to dissolve.

The works are fragments of a larger philosophical landscape. They explore the possibility that what we perceive is never entirely separate from the consciousness through which we perceive it.

In this sense, reflection becomes more than the act of looking at an image. It becomes an encounter with oneself.

REFLECTRE is not a conclusion.
It is an invitation to look again.

MULTIPLICITY

Multiplicity is an invitation to discover more of what you have always been. Human beings are conditioned to think of ourselves as one — one name, one story, one body, one timeline. But this linear, singular self is a functional illusion, useful for survival but too narrow to contain the full depth of who we are.

The self is not a linear straight line. It is a dynamic constellation, a shifting pattern of possible versions. The concept of Multiplicity is not a fantasy, but a real psychological, existential, and spiritual architecture of identity.

It proposes that you are not a single fixed self, but a field of selves including: the remembered self, the performed self, the suppressed self, the imagined self, the cosmic self, and many others.

The intention is not to collapse the many into one single identity. It is to become aware of the many selves within one self, and to learn to navigate these different versions with clarity, compassion, and awareness.

Infinite Chase

Most of the time, achievement begins as an expression of human will—a desire to move beyond what already exists, to transform an idea into reality, and to experience the satisfaction of becoming. Yet, somewhere within this movement, achievement can transform into obsession. What begins as a conscious pursuit gradually becomes a mechanism that pursues us. And so the cycle continues. We accomplish, experience fulfilment for a brief moment, and then begin to Chase again. The human being becomes caught within what may be called the Infinite Chase—a continuous movement toward something that always appears to exist slightly beyond the present moment. We move forward believing that the next achievement will finally provide completion, while completion itself keeps moving further away.

In this process, we become vulnerable to confusing physical fulfilment with existential fulfilment. The body receives the reward. The mind receives recognition. The ego receives confirmation. But the soul may remain untouched. This is where the Infinite Chase begins to distort us. Instead of using achievement as a means of expressing our existence, we begin using existence as a means of producing achievement. Our value becomes attached to what we have accomplished rather than to what we are. Gradually, the Chase begins to define the individual who is doing the Chasing. And when the Chase becomes the source of identity, we begin to lose the authenticity of the soul. The tragedy is not that we desire more. Desire itself is not the problem. Evolution requires movement, curiosity, creation and transformation. The problem emerges when movement loses awareness of its origin.

We forget why we began to move. The Infinite Chase then becomes a loop for the soul. We Chase because we believe achievement will complete us. We achieve, but completion is temporary. The temporary fulfilment produces another desire. The new desire creates another Chase. And the Chase creates another version of ourselves that must be fulfilled. The loop continues. Eventually, what was once an expression of our Will becomes a force that consumes it. This is the beginning of Spiritual Decay. Spiritual decay does not necessarily appear as destruction. It can appear as success. It can appear as productivity, ambition, recognition and endless movement. A person may appear to be progressing externally while becoming increasingly disconnected internally. The greater the distance between what we are becoming and what our soul actually recognizes as authentic, the deeper the distortion becomes.

Nature, however, does not demand that everything be accumulated. Nature operates through cycles, transformation, disappearance and renewal. Nothing exists merely to remain in a permanent state of completion. The seed does not become a tree so that it can endlessly become something else. It becomes a tree because that is the movement inherent within it. Perhaps the human being must learn the same distinction: There is a difference between evolving and endlessly chasing. Evolution emerges from within. The Chase often emerges from the belief that something outside ourselves is missing. This is why Will becomes fundamental. Will is not simply the force that makes us achieve. Will is the ability to consciously choose the direction of our movement. It is the moment in which we stop being carried by the Infinite Chase and become aware of the one who is Chasing. The Will creates the possibility of interruption. It allows us to ask: What am I pursuing? Why am I pursuing it? Who am I becoming through this pursuit? And, if I finally reach it, what remains? These questions create a space between desire and action. Within that space, awareness appears. And awareness becomes the possibility of returning to authenticity. The universe may appear dark when there is no reflection of consciousness within it. Yet the darkness itself is not necessarily empty. It is a space waiting for a spark. hYour Will becomes that spark. Your Reflection becomes the means through which that spark becomes visible. Without Reflection, the Chase remains unconscious. We move, but we do not know why. We accumulate experiences, achievements and identities without recognizing the pattern that connects them. But when Reflection enters the process, the Chase becomes visible. And once the Chase is visible, we are no longer completely trapped within it. The purpose, therefore, is not to destroy ambition or abandon achievement.

It is to transform our relationship with them. Achievement can become an expression rather than an addiction. Movement can become evolution rather than escape. Desire can become creation rather than consumption. And the Infinite Chase can become a path of awareness rather than a prison for the soul. Perhaps the greatest achievement is not reaching the end of the Chase. Perhaps it is realizing that there was never an end to reach. The true evolution begins when the individual stops asking, “What must I achieve next?” and begins asking, “What must I become aware of now?” In that moment, the direction of movement changes. The Chase no longer pulls the soul forward. The soul begins to illuminate the Chase. And within that illumination, the Infinite is no longer something we desperately pursue. It becomes the space through which we learn to exist.

Locus

Locus is the unique position—the unique point of being— from which perception, awareness, and action arise. In a more practical sense, a person’s Locus is not simply where they stand in physical space, but where they stand in awareness.

It can be understood even more simply as the state of consciousness from which a person is experiencing the present moment. Understanding one’s own Locus becomes crucial to the consciousness, to the soul, because action arises from awareness. Before we act, we perceive; before we perceive, we stand somewhere within our own consciousness. That position influences how we interpret what we encounter, how we respond to it, and ultimately, how we move through life.

Locus therefore becomes equally important in navigating the complexity of daily life. It invites us to recognise where we ourselves stand in awareness—and, with discernment, to recognise the Locus from which the people around us perceive and act.

At last, concept of Locus is not merely to ask, "Where am I?" It is to realise and be aware of what and how am i experiencing.

Chronovergence

Chronovergence is the convergence of time within consciousness—the moment when the past, present, and future cease to exist as separate directions and meet within a single experience.

Every moment we inhabit carries traces of what has already been and possibilities of what is yet to come. Memory shapes perception, experience reshapes memory, and anticipation gives direction to action. What we call the present is therefore never entirely present; it is a meeting point where different dimensions of time continuously influence one another.

The lines in Chronovergence emerge and diverge, yet remain connected through a shared field. They resemble the movement of time through consciousness—multiple paths, memories, possibilities, and experiences gathering into moments of convergence before separating again. Chronovergence invites us to recognise that we do not experience time merely by moving through it. We experience it by carrying it. The past is carried within awareness. The future is carried within possibility. And the present is where they meet.

In this convergence, a moment becomes more than a point in time. It becomes a threshold—a place where what has been, what is, and what may become participate in the formation of being. Chronovergence is therefore not the movement of consciousness through time, but the meeting of time within consciousness.

Perhaps this is not an accident. Perhaps the absence of physical protection is itself symbolic. A reflection of the infinite possibilities contained within human consciousness. Your awareness is the key.

Locuspect

Locuspect is the act of examining your Locus before allowing your Locus to determine your reality. If Locus is the position from which perception, awareness, and action arise, then Locuspect becomes the conscious practice of looking at that position itself. Before reacting, you pause. Before judging, you observe. Before acting, you ask where you are acting from. Not What am I thinking? alone, but: From where am I thinking this? From what state of consciousness am I responding? What has positioned me here?

Locuspect begins when we stop treating our present state of consciousness as an objective reality.

A moment of fear can make the world appear threatening.

A moment of anger can make another person appear wrong.

A moment of desire can make something appear necessary.

A moment of clarity can make the same reality appear completely different.

The circumstance may not have changed. The Locus has. This is where Locuspect becomes an action rather than simply an idea. It is the conscious interruption between perception and reaction—a moment in which we examine the position from which we are experiencing before allowing that position to produce an action.Through Locuspect, we begin to ask:

Where am I standing right now?

What is influencing my perception?

Am I responding from awareness or from conditioning?

Is this truly my perception, or simply a pattern I have carried into this moment? If I shift my Locus, what becomes different? The purpose is not to find one permanent or perfect Locus. The Locus continuously moves. What matters is becoming aware of its movement. Autonoesis allows us to recognise the patterns within consciousness. Locuspect allows us to examine where those patterns have placed us. And from that awareness, we gain the possibility of choosing whether to remain there or move. Perhaps this is one of the simplest forms of conscious action:

Pause.

Locate.

Observe.

Choose.

Because the moment you become aware of your Locus, you are no longer completely governed by it. Locuspect is the action of knowing where you stand before you decide where to go.

Autonoesis

Autonoesis is the practice of becoming aware of the way your own consciousness thinks.

Every thought follows a movement.

Every reaction has a pattern.

Every decision carries traces of what has already been experienced, learned, remembered, feared, desired, or conditioned. Yet most of the time, we experience these movements without observing them.

We think, and immediately identify with the thought.

We react, and immediately identify with the reaction.

We judge, and rarely question the consciousness from which the judgement emerged.

Autonoesis begins when we create a distance between the thought and the one who is observing the thought. It is not about stopping thought. It is about becoming conscious of the patterns through which thought occurs.

Why did I react this way?

Why does this particular situation trigger this particular response?

Why do I repeatedly make the same choices?

What am I expecting before something even happens?

What am I carrying into this moment from somewhere else? Through this observation, patterns begin to reveal themselves. And once the patterns become visible, the Locus becomes visible with them. Autonoesis therefore becomes a practice of locating oneself within consciousness. It allows us to recognise the mental, emotional, and perceptual movements that continuously position us somewhere within our own awareness. You cannot recognise your Locus without first observing the consciousness that creates it. The practice is simple, yet difficult:

Observe the thought.

Observe the reaction.

Observe the repetition.

Observe the observer.

Do not immediately change it.

Do not judge it.

First, become aware of it. Because awareness creates distance, and within that distance, choice begins to appear. Perhaps this is where Autonoesis becomes transformative. The moment you recognise that your thoughts are patterns rather than absolute truths, you begin to realise that your consciousness is larger than the patterns moving through it.

You are not every thought that appears.

You are not every reaction that emerges.

You are not every pattern you have learned.

You are the consciousness capable of observing them. And through that observation, you begin to discover where you truly stand. That is the practice of Autonoesis. To know your thoughts is to recognise your patterns. To recognise your patterns is to discover your Locus. And to discover your Locus is to begin becoming conscious of yourself.

Chronocage

Consciousness is inherently capable of Chronovergence. It is not bound, by its nature, to the linear movement of time. Yet the moment consciousness enters physical existence, it begins to inherit its boundaries; the body, memory, ego, and the illusion of linear time. And within these boundaries, something begins to fracture. The self becomes divided into what was, what is, and what will be. Memory turns the past into something unreachable. Expectation turns the future into something distant. Ego gives the present a name and convinces consciousness that this particular version of itself is the only one that exists. Slowly, the continuity of being disappears behind the walls of perception. The child becomes a stranger. The person we once were becomes a memory. The person we may become becomes a possibility. And the present stands between them, believing it is alone. This is Chronocage. Not a cage built from iron, but from moments. Not a prison of walls, but of perception. A temporal and physical confinement of the soul that fragments awareness into isolated identities, making each version of the self appear separate from the other. Yet beneath this fragmentation, something remains continuous.

The self does not truly cease to exist when a moment passes. It only changes its position within the unfolding of existence. Perhaps every version of ourselves is still part of the same whole. each one existing as a different expression of the same consciousness, separated only by the way we experience time. But Chronocage keeps consciousness facing one direction. It makes us look backward through memory and forward through anticipation, while the greater continuity of our being remains hidden beyond the narrow horizon of the present. The cage becomes strongest when we believe it is real. To recognise Chronovergence is to begin seeing through it. to sense that the boundaries between who we were, who we are, and who we are becoming may be nothing more than the architecture of linear perception. Chronocage is therefore the condition of consciousness forgetting its own continuity. It is the moment the infinite begins to perceive itself as finite. And perhaps the greatest prison of time is not that we cannot return to the past or enter the future but that we have forgotten that every version of ourselves was always part of the same existence.

The Process

The Process is the space between who you were and who you are becoming.

We often look at transformation as if it happens in a single moment—as if clarity suddenly arrives, the path becomes visible, and everything begins to make sense. But existence rarely transforms this way.

Most of what changes us happens quietly, gradually, and often without our awareness. The Process represents this movement through the different densities of being. From the darkness where everything feels uncertain, towards the light where things slowly begin to reveal themselves.

The darkness is not the absence of light; it is the part of the journey where light has not yet been understood. Every experience, every mistake, every delay, every loss, every realization becomes part of the process. Nothing is separate from it. Even the moments we wish to escape are shaping the Locus from which we will eventually see ourselves differently.

The Process reminds us that becoming is not about reaching a final version of the self. It is about continuously moving, dissolving, reconstructing and emerging again. What appears to be darkness may simply be the beginning of another form of awareness.

The Process is not the journey towards becoming.

The Process itself is becoming.

Butterfly effect

Perhaps nothing truly happens only once. Every thought, every choice, every movement of consciousness may continue travelling long after the moment in which it was born. What appears as a small disturbance within one Locus may become an entirely different reality somewhere beyond its perception. A thought becomes a decision. A decision becomes a movement. A movement enters another existence. And from there, the ripple continues. We rarely witness the full life of our actions.

A word may disappear from our memory while remaining within someone else’s. A brief encounter may quietly redirect a lifetime. A decision made in a single moment may reach toward futures that the one who made it will never see. Perhaps this is why existence can never be understood as a collection of isolated moments. Entanglement reveals that everything within consciousness is connected; the Butterfly Effect reveals that everything connected is capable of being moved.

The smallest movement can disturb the whole. Like the flutter of a butterfly whose wings seem to alter nothing, yet somewhere beyond the horizon, conditions begin to rearrange themselves. The butterfly does not know the storm. It only moves. Perhaps we are no different. We move through existence believing our actions belong only to us, unaware of the invisible architectures they enter, the lives they touch, the possibilities they awaken, or the futures they quietly alter. The mystery is not whether our actions have consequences. The mystery is where those consequences end. Perhaps they do not end at all. Perhaps every action becomes part of a larger continuity, travelling through relationships, memory, time and consciousness, returning in forms we may never recognize as having begun with us.

This makes the Locus more than a point from which we perceive existence. It becomes a point of disturbance. From every Locus, something is released into the greater field of being.

A thought.

A choice.

A presence.

A silence. And once released, it no longer belongs entirely to us. The Butterfly Effect therefore becomes a reflection on the unseen magnitude of the seemingly insignificant. It reminds us that what appears small may only be small from where we are standing. The butterfly never sees the storm. We may never see the reality we set into motion. And perhaps that is the deepest mystery of existence: We are constantly shaping worlds we will never know we touched.
