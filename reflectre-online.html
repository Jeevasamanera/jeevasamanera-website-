INTRODUCTION

REFLECTRE

REFLECTRE is an ongoing exploration of consciousness, perception, and the relationship between the inner and outer worlds.

Through charcoal, portraiture, symbolism, and atmospheric imagery, the series investigates memory, vulnerability, transformation, and spiritual experience.

Each work emerges from an attempt to observe the invisible structures that shape how we experience ourselves and the world around us.

Each work therefore invites the viewer to pause, observe, and recognise something of their own experience within the image.

02

REFLECTRE does not seek to define consciousness or offer a singular interpretation of existence. Instead, it creates a space for contemplation — a place where perception can become uncertain, where memory can overlap with imagination, and where the boundary between the internal and external begins to dissolve.

The works are fragments of a larger philosophical landscape. They explore the possibility that what we perceive is never entirely separate from the consciousness through which we perceive it.

In this sense, reflection becomes more than the act of looking at an image. It becomes an encounter with oneself.

REFLECTRE is not a conclusion.
It is an invitation to look again.

Multiplicity © 2026 Jeeva Samanera

MULTIPLICITY

Multiplicity is an invitation to discover more of what you have always been. Human beings are conditioned to think of ourselves as one — one name, one story, one body, one timeline. But this linear, singular self is a functional illusion, useful for survival but too narrow to contain the full depth of who we are.

The self is not a linear straight line. It is a dynamic constellation, a shifting pattern of possible versions. The concept of Multiplicity is not a fantasy, but a real psychological, existential, and spiritual architecture of identity.

It proposes that you are not a single fixed self, but a field of selves including: the remembered self, the performed self, the suppressed self, the imagined self, the cosmic self, and many others.

The intention is not to collapse the many into one single identity. It is to become aware of the many selves within one self, and to learn to navigate these different versions with clarity, compassion, and awareness.

Contrast Of Being

Contrast of Being explores the choas between presence and absence, visibility and disappearance, the self and the space surrounding it. we usually get caught between two conditions of existence. One side dissolves into darkness and texture, while the other opens into an almost empty field of light. The contrast suggests that being is never experienced as a singular state.

We become conscious of ourselves through what surrounds us,through what is present as much as through what is absent.The dark form can be under- stood as the accumulated layers of identity, memory, experience, and perception. The empty space, meanwhile, represents the unknown — the space before identity takes form, where consciousness remains open and undefined.

The human presence within the composition becomes almost insignificant against these opposing fields. Yet its very presence creates the contrast through which both darkness and light become perceptible. Perhaps being is not simply existing, but existing between what we know and what we dont or cannot know, between form and formlessness, between the self we recognise and the self that remains hidden. Contrast of Being invites us to observe the space between these opposites, and question yourself; where do you truly begin?

Infinite Chase

Most of the time, achievement begins as an expression of human will—a desire to move beyond what already exists, to transform an idea into reality, and to experience the satisfaction of becoming. Yet, somewhere within this movement, achievement can transform into obsession. What begins as a conscious pursuit gradually becomes a mechanism that pursues us. And so the cycle continues. We accomplish, experience fulfilment for a brief moment, and then begin to Chase again. The human being becomes caught within what may be called the Infinite Chase—a continuous movement toward something that always appears to exist slightly beyond the present moment. We move forward believing that the next achievement will finally provide completion, while completion itself keeps moving further away.

In this process, we become vulnerable to confusing physical fulfilment with existential fulfilment. The body receives the reward. The mind receives recognition. The ego receives confirmation. But the soul may remain untouched. This is where the Infinite Chase begins to distort us. Instead of using achievement as a means of expressing our existence, we begin using existence as a means of producing achievement. Our value becomes attached to what we have accomplished rather than to what we are. Gradually, the Chase begins to define the individual who is doing the Chasing. And when the Chase becomes the source of identity, we begin to lose the authenticity of the soul. The tragedy is not that we desire more. Desire itself is not the problem. Evolution requires movement, curiosity, creation and transformation. The problem emerges when movement loses awareness of its origin.

We forget why we began to move. The Infinite Chase then becomes a loop for the soul. We Chase because we believe achievement will complete us. We achieve, but completion is temporary. The temporary fulfilment produces another desire. The new desire creates another Chase. And the Chase creates another version of ourselves that must be fulfilled. The loop continues. Eventually, what was once an expression of our Will becomes a force that consumes it. This is the beginning of Spiritual Decay. Spiritual decay does not necessarily appear as destruction. It can appear as success. It can appear as productivity, ambition, recognition and endless movement. A person may appear to be progressing externally while becoming increasingly disconnected internally. The greater the distance between what we are becoming and what our soul actually recognizes as authentic, the deeper the distortion becomes.

Nature, however, does not demand that everything be accumulated. Nature operates through cycles, transformation, disappearance and renewal. Nothing exists merely to remain in a permanent state of completion. The seed does not become a tree so that it can endlessly become something else. It becomes a tree because that is the movement inherent within it. Perhaps the human being must learn the same distinction: There is a difference between evolving and endlessly chasing. Evolution emerges from within. The Chase often emerges from the belief that something outside ourselves is missing. This is why Will becomes fundamental. Will is not simply the force that makes us achieve. Will is the ability to consciously choose the direction of our movement. It is the moment in which we stop being carried by the Infinite Chase and become aware of the one who is Chasing. The Will creates the possibility of interruption. It allows us to ask: What am I pursuing? Why am I pursuing it? Who am I becoming through this pursuit? And, if I finally reach it, what remains? These questions create a space between desire and action. Within that space, awareness appears. And awareness becomes the possibility of returning to authenticity. The universe may appear dark when there is no reflection of consciousness within it. Yet the darkness itself is not necessarily empty. It is a space waiting for a spark. hYour Will becomes that spark. Your Reflection becomes the means through which that spark becomes visible. Without Reflection, the Chase remains unconscious. We move, but we do not know why. We accumulate experiences, achievements and identities without recognizing the pattern that connects them. But when Reflection enters the process, the Chase becomes visible. And once the Chase is visible, we are no longer completely trapped within it. The purpose, therefore, is not to destroy ambition or abandon achievement.

It is to transform our relationship with them. Achievement can become an expression rather than an addiction. Movement can become evolution rather than escape. Desire can become creation rather than consumption. And the Infinite Chase can become a path of awareness rather than a prison for the soul. Perhaps the greatest achievement is not reaching the end of the Chase. Perhaps it is realizing that there was never an end to reach. The true evolution begins when the individual stops asking, “What must I achieve next?” and begins asking, “What must I become aware of now?” In that moment, the direction of movement changes. The Chase no longer pulls the soul forward. The soul begins to illuminate the Chase. And within that illumination, the Infinite is no longer something we desperately pursue. It becomes the space through which we learn to exist.

Sometimes, what we perceive as being held back is life giving us moment to become aware, to learn, to strengthen your will, or to make you realise what you are really asking for. Sacred Delay is therefore not an absence of movements. It is movement occurring beneath your awareness because what appears to be waiting may, in fact, preparation of the soul.

Locus

Locus is the unique position—the unique point of being— from which perception, awareness, and action arise. In a more practical sense, a person’s Locus is not simply where they stand in physical space, but where they stand in awareness.

It can be understood even more simply as the state of consciousness from which a person is experiencing the present moment. Understanding one’s own Locus becomes crucial to the consciousness, to the soul, because action arises from awareness. Before we act, we perceive; before we perceive, we stand somewhere within our own consciousness. That position influences how we interpret what we encounter, how we respond to it, and ultimately, how we move through life.

Locus therefore becomes equally important in navigating the complexity of daily life. It invites us to recognise where we ourselves stand in awareness—and, with discernment, to recognise the Locus from which the people around us perceive and act.

At last, concept of Locus is not merely to ask, "Where am I?" It is to realise and be aware of what and how am i experiencing.

Chronovergence © 2026 Jeeva Samanera
18
Chronovergence

Chronovergence is the convergence of time within consciousness—the moment when the past, present, and future cease to exist as separate directions and meet within a single experience.

Every moment we inhabit carries traces of what has already been and possibilities of what is yet to come. Memory shapes perception, experience reshapes memory, and anticipation gives direction to action. What we call the present is therefore never entirely present; it is a meeting point where different dimensions of time continuously influence one another.

The lines in Chronovergence emerge and diverge, yet remain connected through a shared field. They resemble the movement of time through consciousness—multiple paths, memories, possibilities, and experiences gathering into moments of convergence before separating again. Chronovergence invites us to recognise that we do not experience time merely by moving through it. We experience it by carrying it. The past is carried within awareness. The future is carried within possibility. And the present is where they meet.

In this convergence, a moment becomes more than a point in time. It becomes a threshold—a place where what has been, what is, and what may become participate in the formation of being. Chronovergence is therefore not the movement of consciousness through time, but the meeting of time within consciousness.

Yet With You proposes a contradiction to this fear:

You are never truly alone. You are always accompanied by yourself—not merely by the self you recognize, but by a higher self existing beyond the limitations of your present perception.Your higher self is the dimension of you that sees beyond the immediate moment. It is the unseen intelligence that can guide you through possibilities you cannot yet perceive, opening paths where none seemed to exist and, at times, bending the very reality around you toward what you are becoming.

What first appears to be coincidence gradually reveals itself as something more profound.

Coincidence becomes synchronicity.

Synchronicity becomes serendipity.

Serendipity begins to resemble fate. And fate, when consciously lived, can become destiny.

Perhaps this is the kairolume moment—the moment in which you recognize that fate is not simply something that happens to you. It is also something you participate in creating.

It is you who chooses.

It is you who responds.

It is you who walks through the doors when they appear.

Your higher self does not necessarily remove the uncertainty of life; it accompanies you through it. It becomes the silent guardian within you, continually opening doors toward infinite possibilities.

The guardian angel you have been searching for may not be somewhere above you.

It have been you, all along.

It is You, always with You.

Entanglement

Entanglement explores the invisible threads that bind one existence to another. Within its movements, forms appear to emerge, dissolve, and return. Suggesting that nothing truly exists in isolation.

Every thought touches another thought. Every action leaves an imprint. Every experience becomes part of something larger than itself.

The central form becomes a passage through this invisible network. A point where energies converge, intertwine, and transform. The surrounding spirals evoke thoughts, memories, relationships, choices, and consequences continuously moving through consciousness, like ripples travelling through an unseen ocean.

In this sense, entanglement is the hidden architecture of karma. Every action sends a movement into the field, and that movement eventually finds its way back, not always as a direct consequence, but through unexpected encounters, repeating patterns, unlikely coin-ci-dences, and moments that seem to arrive precisely when they are needed. These are the moments when entanglement rises to the surface as Kairolume, when the invisible relationships between past actions, present awareness, and unfolding circumstance briefly become luminous and perceptible.

A meeting with a person happens at the right moment that feels strangely inevitable.

A forgotten memory returns through an unexpected encounter.

A coincidence carries the weight ofsomething that feels strangely inevitable.

Entanglement is the visible emergence of the invisible knots in the fabric of the reality, which is the karmic movements travelling through consciousness and converging within a particular mommentor experience.

what we call may simply be entanglement revealing a connection we were previously unable to see. Karma is its movements. Kairolume is the moment it becomes visible.

Luminescence

Every person carries an aura that is more than what the eye can see.

There are moments when someone seems to become luminous. Not because their appearance changes, but because their presence does. Their being feels clearer, deeper, almost as if something within them has begun to radiate outward.

Luminescence is this radiance of an awakened Locus.

The person becomes conscious of where they stand within themselves, they begin to understand the shifting layers of their Multiplicity and the opposing forces that shape their Contrast of Being. With this understanding, the fragments of the self slowly begin to align. And when this alignment reaches toward the Higher Selves, perception changes.

This alignment creates a different kind of presence. It is as if the person starts carrying a certain frequency that can be felt beyond what is physically visible. Their thoughts, actions, choices and surroundings begin to correspond with one another in ways that feel increasingly meaningful.

This is where synchronicity begins to reveal itself. What first appears as coincidence may gradually become synchronicity. Synchronicity may then open into serendipity, where unexpected events begin to arrive with an almost precise relevance to one's path. The person does not necessarily create these events. They simply become clear enough to recognise them.

Luminescence is therefore not about becoming brighter than others. It is about becoming more aligned with oneself. The clearer the Locus becomes, the more the Multiplicity is understood, the more the Contrast of Being is embraced, and the closer one moves toward the Higher Selves, the more reality begins to reflect that alignment.

Luminescence is the visible aura of an invisible alignment.

A coincidence carries the weight ofsomething that feels strangely inevitable.
